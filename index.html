Each template gets transcribed many times over. That amplification is what makes the readout bright.

T7 RNA polymerase needs a double-stranded promoter to start. It doesn't need a double-stranded template to keep going. That's why a gate that's single-stranded almost everywhere still works. The promoter here is T7 Max, and standard T7 RNA polymerase reads it.

Reference

Enzyme
PvuII. Recognition site CAGCTG, 6 base pairs. Blunt cut between CAG and CTG, leaving no overhangs. measured, Sharon et al. 2023
Of the eight restriction enzymes screened for this platform, four (PvuII, BsaAI, NruI, RsaI) digested only double-stranded templates. measured, Sharon et al. 2023
Reactions run in aHOT 7.9, a custom buffer built so that restriction digestion, polymerase extension and cell-free transcription all work in one system. measured, Sharon et al. 2023
Reporter
Broccoli aptamer + DFHBI dye. Excitation 472 nm, emission 507 nm. measured, Sharon et al. 2023 DFHBI is the fluorophore. On its own it is barely fluorescent, and it lights up once the folded aptamer holds it. The aptamer is the activator.
Reported result
The 1 signal is about 90× the 0 signal. measured, Sharon et al. 2023 So a 0 keeps roughly 1% of the 1 signal.
Template
A single strand of 105 nucleotides: the 26-base T7 Max promoter, a 12-base flanking region, the CAGCTG site at bases 39–44, another 12-base flanking region, then a 49-base aptamer coding region. Inputs are 15 bases — 12 flanking bases plus 3 bases of the site. measured, Sharon et al. 2023, SI Fig. 3
The Methods section prints 10 + 6 + 10 with 13-base inputs for this same gate, which is in fact the NOT gate's architecture. Only 12 + 6 + 12 sums to the 105 nucleotides reported, and the sequence in SI Fig. 3 confirms it. Sharon et al. 2023, Methods vs. SI Fig. 3
T7 Max is a higher-yield T7 promoter variant from the same lab (Deich et al. 2023, J Biol Eng 17:4). Standard T7 RNA polymerase recognizes it, so no engineered enzyme is required.
This demo's pacing
Roughly 2–3 seconds per step, and a handful of polymerase passes per run. illustrative Nothing here is scaled to real time, and no rates or concentrations are modelled.

How the real assay runs

The real assay runs in two stages. Reaction A is a cooling program that runs from 95 °C down to 37 °C at about 5 °C per minute, annealing the strands on the way down, then holds at 37 °C for about 15 minutes to digest. Reaction B is a cell-free transcription reaction, read at 37 °C every 30 minutes over 5+ hours. measured, Sharon et al. 2023 Inputs cannot be added part-way through the readout. This demo compresses both stages so the mechanism is visible in a few seconds.

Trumpet has a second family of gates (AND and OR) that work by DNA polymerase extension instead of restriction digestion. This demo covers the restriction-enzyme family only.

A real gate is single-use. Cut templates aren't regenerated, and template regeneration is named in the paper as a remaining milestone. Reset here is a teaching convenience. The chemistry has no equivalent.

The template is the antisense strand; the promoter oligo and the two inputs are its sense complements. The zones above are labelled by function instead.
